1. Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning

Standard reinforcement learning policies aim to maximize the expected sum of future rewards. Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) modifies this objective by augmenting it with a term that rewards the policy for being **diverse** and **exploratory**. This framework is known as Maximum Entropy RL.

The generalized objective function \(J(\pi)\) is:

\[J(\pi) = \sum_{t=0}^{T} \mathbb{E}_{(s_t, a_t) \sim \rho_\pi} \left[ R(s_t, a_t) + \alpha \mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot | s_t)) \right]\]

Where:

  • \(\mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot | s_t)) = -\sum_a \pi(a|s_t) \log \pi(a|s_t)\) is the **Entropy** of the action distribution. Higher entropy means the policy acts more randomly, exploring different paths.
  • \(\alpha\) is the **Temperature Parameter**, balancing exploration (high entropy) and exploitation (maximizing reward).

This formulation makes MASAC extremely robust to premature policy collapse. Instead of converging quickly to a single sub-optimal strategy, agents explore multiple action trajectories to seek higher reward peaks.

2. Automatic Temperature Tuning (\(\alpha\))

Manually tuning a fixed \(\alpha\) parameter is notoriously difficult: early in training we want high exploration (high \(\alpha\)), but late in training we want the policy to exploit learned behaviors (low \(\alpha\)).

MASAC solves this by treating entropy as a constraint problem. We want to maximize rewards while keeping the entropy of the policy above a target threshold \(\bar{H}\) (typically \(-\text{dim}(\mathcal{A})\)). The temperature \(\alpha\) is dynamically updated at each gradient step by minimizing the loss:

\[J(\alpha) = \mathbb{E}_{a_t \sim \pi_t} \left[ -\alpha \log \pi_t(a_t | s_t) - \alpha \bar{H} \right]\]

3. Off-Policy MASAC & Replay Buffers

In contrast to MAPPO (which is on-policy and discards data immediately), MASAC is an **Off-Policy** algorithm. It decouples simulation from learning using a **Replay Buffer**.

STEP 1: DATA SAMPLING
Decentralized Actor

Samples active moves in the JAX environment.

→
Replay Buffer

Stores transition tuples \((s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}, d_t)\). Holds up to 100,000 steps.

STEP 2: GRADIENT UPDATE
Mini-batch Sample

Randomly selects 256 transition steps from buffer.

→
Centralized Q-Critics

Compute temporal-difference (TD) losses to update Actor & Critic weights.

Figure 1: Off-policy training pipeline in MASAC. Simulations feed the Replay Buffer; network updates draw batches randomly from it.

Decoupling data gathering from learning yields high **sample efficiency**, allowing MASAC to run many gradient updates per environment step. However, keeping historical trajectories under non-stationarity (other agents changing behavior) can introduce training instability.

4. Architectural Training Loop & Component Flow

Below is the step-by-step update process within MASAC's training pipeline:

1
Decentralized Data Sampling

Actors run in parallel, writing transition tuples \((s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}, d_t)\) into a shared off-policy Replay Buffer.

2
Mini-batch Sampling

A random batch of transitions is sampled from the Replay Buffer to break temporal correlations.

3
Joint Q-value TD Estimation

Centralized Critics estimate \(Q(S_t, \mathbf{a}_t)\) for the joint action. Target critics calculate standard Bellman targets.

4
Actor Loss with Entropy

Actor networks are updated by maximizing expected Q-values plus the policy entropy term \(\alpha \mathcal{H}(\pi)\).

5
Temperature update

The temperature parameter \(\alpha\) is updated to maintain the target entropy threshold.

6
Soft Target Network Updates

Target networks undergo slow soft updates (\(\theta^- \leftarrow \tau \theta + (1-\tau)\theta^-\)) to stabilize TD-target estimation.

5. Double Q-Critics and Centralization

Since MASAC is a Q-Learning derivative, it uses Q-Networks \(Q(s, a)\) rather than State-Value networks \(V(s)\). To address overestimation bias (a common problem where Q-values are artificially inflated), MASAC employs **Double Critic Networks** (\(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\)) and target networks.

During training, the target value for updating the Q-networks is computed as:

\[y = r + \gamma \left( \min_{i=1,2} Q_{\theta_i^-}(s', a') - \alpha \log \pi(a'|s') \right)\]

Where:

  • \(Q_{\theta_i^-}\) are the **Target Critic Networks**.
  • The \(\min\) operator prevents positive feedback loops that inflate expected rewards.
  • The log-probability term subtracts entropy cost, scaling the target based on the current exploration factor.

Target networks are updated slowly using **Soft Updates (Polyak Averaging)**:

\[\theta^- \leftarrow \tau \theta + (1 - \tau) \theta^- \quad (\tau \approx 0.005)\]

6. MASAC in Lux AI Season 3

For Lux AI Season 3, MASAC's entropy-based exploration acts as an excellent driver for finding hidden relic nodes without needing extremely dense rewards. However, the off-policy replay buffer introduces coordination lag: historical transitions might contain moves of ships that have since been destroyed or changed their behavior patterns, requiring careful hyperparameter tuning (like smaller buffer sizes or lower learning rates).
